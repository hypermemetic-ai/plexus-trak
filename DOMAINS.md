# Domain Name Candidates

For the unified platform: code management (hyperforge) + work management (trak) + whatever comes next.

## Criteria

- 6 characters
- Easy to type, easy to spell
- Deep mathematical/CS reference that captures recursion
- Nobody would have seen it as a product name

## Candidates

### torsor
A group that forgot where its origin is. Every point is equivalent — no privileged center. The structure is defined purely by relationships between elements, not by any distinguished element. In a torsor, you can measure the *difference* between any two points, but no single point is "zero."

**Why it fits:** Facets have no inherent hierarchy. No root is special. Any facet is a valid viewport. The platform's structure IS the relationships — containment, links, forks — not any fixed top-down ordering. You're always in the middle of a torsor, never at an edge.

**Domain:** `torsor.dev`, `torsor.io`

---

### yoneda
The Yoneda lemma (category theory): "An object is completely determined by its relationships to all other objects." You don't need to know what a thing IS — you only need to know how it maps to everything else. Identity is relational, not intrinsic.

**Why it fits:** A facet is defined by its children, its links, its comments, its forks, its refs. Strip those away and there's nothing left. The Yoneda perspective is the exact architecture: facets are addresses, services provide aspects, identity emerges from the constellation.

**Domain:** `yoneda.dev`, `yoneda.io`

---

### operad
An algebraic structure encoding how operations compose. An operad operation takes other operad operations as inputs, recursively. The theory of "things that plug into things that plug into things." Operads formalize the idea that composition itself has structure.

**Why it fits:** Facets contain facets. Agents spawn agents. Builds compose builds. The platform is a recursive composition engine — not just data, but the operations over data compose the same way. An operad is the algebra of recursive pluggability.

**Domain:** `operad.dev`, `operad.io`

---

### dessin
Short for "dessin d'enfant" — a child's drawing. Grothendieck discovered that simple bipartite graphs drawn on surfaces encode the absolute Galois group (one of the deepest objects in mathematics). The surface looks like a child scribbled on it, but underneath is infinite algebraic structure.

**Why it fits:** The UI is simple — boxes, lines, trees. But underneath is a recursive graph with typed edges, version-controlled forks, dependency analysis, and agent orchestration. Simple surface, infinite depth. A child could draw the diagram; a Fields medalist couldn't exhaust its structure.

**Domain:** `dessin.dev`, `dessin.io`

---

### hensel
Hensel's lemma: if you have an approximate solution at one level of precision, you can *lift* it to the next level. Solutions propagate upward through recursive refinement. You solve locally, then lift globally.

**Why it fits:** You start with a rough facet ("build the thing"), refine it into subtasks, refine those into sub-subtasks. Each level lifts the solution from the level below. The platform is a Hensel lifting engine — rough plans become precise execution through recursive decomposition.

**Domain:** `hensel.dev`, `hensel.io`

---

### menger
The Menger sponge: start with a cube, punch holes in every face, then punch holes in every face of what remains, forever. The result has infinite surface area but zero volume. It is *all boundary* — every point is on the edge.

**Why it fits:** In a fractal work system, every facet is a boundary — between done and not-done, between this agent's work and that agent's work, between the plan and the code. There is no interior. You are always at the edge of what's known, looking into what isn't.

**Domain:** `menger.dev`, `menger.io`
